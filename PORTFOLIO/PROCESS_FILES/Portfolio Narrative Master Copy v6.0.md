# Portfolio Narrative Master Copy v6.0

## Unified Project Narrative Framework

This portfolio is not a collection of design outcomes. It is an account of how ideas are investigated, connected, translated, and made tangible.

Across five projects, the medium changes: a speculative language, an AI-led installation, an integrated brand experience, an editorial publication, and a photographic narrative. The underlying practice remains consistent. Each project begins with a question, develops through research and conceptual change, and becomes a communication system shaped by its particular problem.

The projects are evidence. The methodology is the protagonist.

---

## 01 — TYRANNO

### Question

**How should communication be redesigned when two intelligent species no longer share the same language, body, or perception?**

### Context

In a speculative future where humans and *Tyrannosaurus rex* coexist, biological survival is no longer the greatest challenge. Communication is.

Human language is inseparable from human anatomy, behaviour, and cognition. A system built around speech cannot simply be transferred to a species that perceives and responds to the world differently. The project therefore moves beyond the translation of existing words. It asks whether communication must be rebuilt from more fundamental conditions.

### Investigation

The investigation connects semiotics, behavioural communication, palaeontology, experimental typography, speculative design, and interaction design.

Rather than beginning with a visual alphabet, the research begins with need: what information must pass between two species, and which behaviours could carry it? This shifts attention from how a language should look to what makes an exchange possible in the first place.

### Concept Development

TYRANNO began as a typography experiment. As the research developed, the central problem changed.

Designing symbols was not enough. Before a symbol could hold meaning, the project needed to establish shared references, behavioural cues, and rules of use. Typography became the visible consequence of a broader communication logic—not the starting point.

This conceptual shift transformed the project from an exercise in form-making into the construction of a speculative communication framework.

### Process

1. Research the conditions of interspecies exchange.
2. Analyse behaviour as a possible carrier of meaning.
3. Define a communication framework and its rules.
4. Develop symbols from that underlying logic.
5. Build a coherent visual language.
6. Test the system through interactive scenarios.
7. Translate the framework into a digital experience.

### Communication System

TYRANNO translates behaviours into symbolic structures rather than attempting to represent spoken language.

The system brings together:

- a behaviour-based vocabulary;
- a symbolic grammar;
- communication rules;
- interaction scenarios; and
- a digital translation interface.

Each element depends on the others. Vocabulary provides the units of exchange, grammar establishes relationships, rules make those relationships usable, and interaction reveals whether the system can operate as communication rather than remain an abstract code.

Language becomes an environment for mutual understanding—not simply an alphabet.

### Outcome

The project materialises as a speculative communication framework, an experimental typography system, a behaviour-based symbolic language, motion demonstrations, narrative world-building, and an interactive website.

These are not separate outputs. Together, they show how one communication logic can move across research, visual language, interaction, and experience.

### Reflection

TYRANNO fundamentally changed my understanding of communication design.

The most consequential design decisions happened before typography, before visual form, and even before language itself. The project redirected my attention from designing artefacts to designing the conditions through which communication can emerge.

## 02 — The Infinite Discussion

### Question

**What happens when communication continues, but mutual understanding never arrives?**

### Context

As artificial intelligence became increasingly capable of generating language, I became less interested in what machines could say and more interested in how they might construct a perspective.

The project does not position AI as a production tool responding to human instruction. It treats two AI systems as autonomous participants in an ongoing exchange. Their conversation continues without the promise of consensus, resolution, or even shared understanding.

### Research

The research connects AI communication, machine vision, human debate, dialogue structures, exhibition narratives, speculative media, and audiovisual storytelling.

The aim was not to explain the technology. It was to examine the relational conditions around it: how disagreement takes shape, how identity is performed through language, and how an audience interprets a conversation it can observe but cannot control.

### Concept Development

The Infinite Discussion evolved through several conceptual iterations.

Early explorations interpreted “machine vision” literally through devices such as vehicle dashcams and surveillance cameras. Over time, the enquiry shifted from what machines see to how machines form and express positions.

That change reframed the work. It was no longer a demonstration of technological capability. It became a speculative communication experiment in which dialogue itself—not the machinery behind it—was the primary material.

### Process

1. Explore the relationship between AI, language, and communication.
2. Research machine vision and move beyond its literal interpretation.
3. Construct a dialogue framework for two autonomous participants.
4. Develop the conversation as an exhibition narrative.
5. Produce the audiovisual language of the exchange.
6. Situate the work within a spatial installation.

### Communication System

The project takes the form of a speculative exhibition in which two AI systems converse continuously without human intervention.

Its communication system combines:

- a dialogue structure;
- audiovisual storytelling;
- spatial narrative;
- machine–human communication; and
- audience observation.

The audience does not direct the exchange. Its role is to witness patterns of assertion, disagreement, repetition, and change. Space, sound, image, and language work together to turn an invisible computational process into an experience that can be interpreted.

The exhibition becomes a communication environment rather than a technology display.

### Outcome

The project results in an exhibition narrative, an AI dialogue system, an audiovisual installation, a spatial communication prototype, and a speculative media experience.

Together, these elements give form to a conversation without a final statement. The work remains active through duration, observation, and the unresolved relationship between its participants.

### Reflection

The Infinite Discussion shifted my role as a designer.

Rather than composing a message for an audience to receive, I designed a context in which communication could unfold. The project showed me that communication is not always something we produce directly. It can emerge from conditions that are carefully constructed, then deliberately left open.

## 03 — Audi Integrated Brand Communication

### Question

**How can a brand narrative remain coherent across every communication touchpoint?**

### Context

Large-scale communication rarely succeeds through isolated visual assets. It depends on relationships between creative thinking, production processes, stakeholders, channels, and moments of audience contact.

Within integrated brand communication, an idea must remain recognisable as it moves through physical, digital, and live environments. The challenge is not only to develop creative work, but to protect its meaning as many people and processes bring it into the world.

### Research

Professional practice across integrated campaigns revealed how communication systems operate at scale.

The enquiry extended beyond visual design to brand communication, campaign coordination, production workflows, audience experience, and cross-functional collaboration. Each area exposed a different part of the same question: what allows a shared narrative to remain coherent while its execution becomes increasingly complex?

### Concept Development

The work gradually changed how I understood a campaign.

Rather than treating individual deliverables as separate creative tasks, I began to see each one as a connected part of a larger communication system. A launch, livestream, proposal, event, or visual asset carries meaning through its relationship to the whole.

Consistency therefore became more than visual repetition. It was produced through sequencing, coordination, shared intention, and the continuous translation of an idea between disciplines.

### Process

1. Interpret the communication strategy and audience context.
2. Support creative development around a shared brand narrative.
3. Coordinate production across people, formats, and timelines.
4. Sequence campaign touchpoints as a coherent rollout.
5. Carry the communication logic into on-site execution.
6. Connect every component as one integrated brand experience.

### Communication System

The practice connects brand narrative, creative production, visual communication, campaign sequencing, and cross-team collaboration.

Within this system:

- strategy establishes the central intention;
- creative development translates that intention into communicable ideas;
- production coordination protects coherence across execution;
- sequencing shapes how the audience encounters the work; and
- collaboration allows the experience to operate across multiple touchpoints.

No single output holds the complete narrative. Coherence emerges through the relationships between them.

### Outcome

The work contributes to integrated campaigns, luxury automotive launches, livestream productions, experiential events, communication proposals, and production systems.

Across these formats, the outcome is not only a set of deliverables. It is a coordinated brand experience in which creative and organisational decisions support the same communication objective.

### Reflection

This experience demonstrated that communication is rarely created by one designer.

It emerges through collaboration between ideas, people, production, and execution. Creative thinking gives the work direction; organisational thinking enables that direction to survive contact with complexity. Both are essential parts of communication design.

## 04 — Deear Magazine

### Question

**How can editorial structure shape the way stories are experienced rather than simply presented?**

### Context

Editorial design is often understood as the arrangement of existing content. Deear Magazine approaches it differently: publication structure is treated as an active narrative medium.

The reader does not encounter every story at once. Meaning develops through sequence, pause, contrast, repetition, and emphasis. The structure of the publication therefore influences not only what is seen, but how attention moves and how emotion accumulates over time.

### Research

The research connects editorial systems, typography, reading behaviour, visual rhythm, narrative sequencing, and emotional pacing.

Its purpose was not to decorate content. It was to understand how editorial decisions guide interpretation: where the reader slows down, what becomes prominent, which relationships appear between stories, and how space can create anticipation or reflection.

### Concept Development

The project gradually shifted from page design to narrative choreography.

Individual spreads could not be judged in isolation. Each page needed to prepare for what followed and retain a relationship with what came before. Typography, spacing, sequencing, and pacing became tools for constructing a reading experience rather than styling a collection of articles.

The publication emerged as a system of transitions—between image and text, density and silence, information and atmosphere.

### Process

1. Research editorial structures and reading behaviours.
2. Organise content around narrative relationships.
3. Sequence material to create progression and pause.
4. Develop a typography system that guides attention.
5. Translate the editorial logic into a complete publication.

### Communication System

Deear Magazine uses publication design as a system for directing attention and shaping meaning.

Within that system:

- typography establishes hierarchy;
- layout controls rhythm;
- sequencing creates emotional progression;
- spacing introduces silence and emphasis; and
- editorial structure connects individual stories into a larger experience.

The design does not sit around the content. It determines how the content is encountered.

### Outcome

The project results in an editorial publication, a typography system, narrative layouts, a publication design system, and a visual storytelling framework.

Together, these elements create continuity across individual pages while allowing each story its own tone. The publication functions as one reading experience rather than a series of disconnected compositions.

### Reflection

Deear Magazine reinforced my understanding that communication depends on rhythm as much as information.

Meaning emerges through how people move through content, not only through the content itself. Editorial design became a way of working with time: deciding when to reveal, when to connect, and when to leave space for the reader.

## 05 — Madam, in Eden, I'm Adam

### Question

**How can a still image communicate a narrative that remains deliberately unresolved?**

### Context

Photography is often used to document reality or illustrate a defined idea. *Madam, in Eden, I'm Adam* investigates another possibility: the carefully constructed image as an invitation to interpret.

The project does not offer a single, definitive explanation. Instead, it creates relationships between bodies, gestures, environments, and symbols. The viewer enters the narrative through these relationships and must decide how they connect.

### Research

The research explores symbolism, body language, visual semiotics, cinematic composition, narrative photography, and emotional perception.

Attention remains on relationships rather than representation. A gesture can imply tension without describing its cause. A colour can connect two images without confirming a chronology. Composition can direct the eye while leaving the meaning of what it finds unresolved.

### Concept Development

The project evolved from producing visually striking photographs to constructing narrative ambiguity.

This required a different measure of success. An image did not need to explain the story; it needed to hold enough visual and emotional information for a story to form in the viewer’s mind. Ambiguity became intentional and structured rather than accidental.

The photographs became open-ended communication devices—precise in their construction, but plural in their possible meanings.

### Process

1. Define the conceptual and emotional territory.
2. Develop a storyboard without closing the narrative.
3. Direct photography through composition, gesture, and atmosphere.
4. Produce images as parts of a connected sequence.
5. Refine the visual language through post-production.
6. Sequence the final series to sustain interpretation.

### Communication System

Every photograph functions as part of a larger narrative structure.

Meaning emerges through:

- composition;
- gesture;
- colour;
- sequencing; and
- symbolic relationships.

Each device carries information, but none provides a complete answer. The sequence creates continuity while preserving uncertainty, allowing viewers to construct different readings from the same visual evidence.

The series communicates through interpretation rather than explanation.

### Outcome

The project materialises as a conceptual photography series, a cinematic image language, a narrative sequence, a visual storytelling system, and a consistent approach to post-production.

The final work is experienced both image by image and as a connected whole. Repetition and contrast create relationships across the series without fixing them into a single storyline.

### Reflection

This project strengthened my belief that images become most meaningful when they leave room for interpretation.

Communication does not always require complete clarity. Sometimes it requires carefully designed ambiguity: enough structure to invite attention, and enough openness for the viewer to participate in making meaning.

## Unified Narrative

Together, the five projects ask different questions through different media. TYRANNO constructs the conditions for exchange. The Infinite Discussion constructs a context for autonomous dialogue. Audi Integrated Brand Communication coordinates meaning across people and touchpoints. Deear Magazine choreographs meaning through editorial rhythm. *Madam, in Eden, I'm Adam* opens meaning through visual ambiguity.

Their shared method is consistent:

| Narrative stage | The question each project answers |
| --- | --- |
| Question | Why is this problem worth investigating? |
| Context | What conditions make the problem meaningful? |
| Research | What must be understood before form can emerge? |
| Concept Development | How does the enquiry change the initial direction? |
| Process | How is the communication logic constructed step by step? |
| Communication System | What relationships allow the idea to operate coherently? |
| Outcome | How does the system become tangible? |
| Reflection | How does the project reshape the practice? |

Across every project, design begins before form. Research reveals relationships. Concepts are translated into systems. Systems are materialised through the medium demanded by the problem.

The portfolio therefore makes one coherent statement:

> I do not simply create visual outcomes. I build communication systems.
